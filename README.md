# TEXT REUSE DETECTOR

**NLP-based text similarity analysis and text reuse detection**

**Author:** Irene Cipparone
**Year:** 2026

---

## Overview

**TEXT REUSE DETECTOR** is an NLP-based prototype for analyzing the degree of similarity between two texts and interpreting the results.

The system takes:

* **Text A:** the original text
* **Text B:** the text to be checked

and calculates two complementary lexical similarity measures:

* **Jaccard Similarity**
* **Cosine Similarity using TF-IDF**

The two scores are then combined into an **Overall Similarity** score.

The system also identifies shared words and provides a qualitative interpretation of the similarity between the texts.

> The prototype is designed to provide a quantitative indication of textual similarity. It does not make an absolute judgment of plagiarism.

---

## Objectives

The project investigates how different forms of text reuse affect automatically measured lexical similarity.

The experimental dataset includes the following conditions:

* unrelated text;
* literal copy;
* near-copy;
* lexical substitution with synonyms;
* syntactic reformulation;
* paraphrase;
* LLM-generated paraphrase.

The main objective is to understand how effectively simple NLP similarity measures capture these different types of textual transformation.

## Method

### 1. Preprocessing

Both texts undergo linguistic preprocessing using **spaCy**.

The pipeline includes:

1. lowercasing;
2. tokenization;
3. selection of alphabetic tokens;
4. lemmatization;
5. stopword removal.

### 2. Text Representation

After preprocessing, texts are represented using **unigrams (1-grams)**.

### 3. Similarity Measures

Two complementary similarity measures are calculated.

#### Jaccard Similarity

Jaccard measures the overlap between the sets of words contained in the two texts:

```text
J(A,B) = |A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|
```

It focuses on the proportion of shared vocabulary.

#### Cosine Similarity with TF-IDF

The texts are represented as TF-IDF vectors based on unigrams and compared using cosine similarity.

This measure considers not only whether terms are shared, but also their relative importance within the texts.

### 4. Overall Similarity

The final similarity score is calculated as the arithmetic mean of the two measures:

```text
Overall Similarity =
(Jaccard + Cosine TF-IDF) / 2
```

The result is expressed as a percentage.

## Output

For each pair of texts, the system produces:

* Jaccard similarity (%);
* Cosine TF-IDF similarity (%);
* Overall similarity (%);
* number of shared words;
* list of shared words;
* qualitative similarity profile;
* explanation of the relationship between the two similarity measures.

The system also performs an **error analysis** to identify problematic cases.

---

## Experimental Dataset

The experimental dataset contains an original text and several versions generated through controlled transformations.

| Condition | Type of text reuse              |
| --------- | ------------------------------- |
| 0%        | Unrelated text                  |
| 100%      | Literal copy                    |
| 90%       | Near-copy                       |
| 80%       | Lexical substitution / synonyms |
| 70%       | Syntactic reformulation         |
| 60%       | Paraphrase                      |
| 20%       | LLM-generated paraphrase        |

These controlled conditions allow the behavior of the similarity measures to be compared across different degrees and types of textual transformation.

The condition labels are used as **experimental ground truth** for evaluation and error analysis. They are not predictions generated by the similarity engine.

---

## Interpretation

The system does not rely on a single fixed threshold to determine whether a text is plagiarized.

Instead, the final score is interpreted together with:

* the individual Jaccard and Cosine TF-IDF scores;
* the difference between the two measures;
* the shared vocabulary;
* the experimental condition;
* the results of the error analysis.

This makes it possible to investigate cases where lexical overlap may provide misleading evidence of textual reuse.

---

## Error Analysis

The project includes two main types of error analysis.

### False Positive Candidates

These are texts labelled as **unrelated** in the experimental dataset but receiving relatively high similarity scores.

They can reveal cases where common vocabulary produces apparently high similarity despite the absence of actual textual reuse.

### False Negative Candidates

These are texts belonging to a **reuse condition** but receiving relatively low similarity scores.

They are particularly relevant for transformations such as paraphrasing, synonym substitution and LLM-generated rewriting, where the underlying meaning may be preserved while lexical overlap decreases.

The shared words in problematic cases are also inspected to provide a linguistic interpretation of the results.

---

## Limitations

The current system primarily measures **lexical similarity**.

Consequently, it may have difficulty detecting:

* deep semantic paraphrases;
* extensive synonym substitution;
* substantial syntactic reformulation;
* LLM-generated rewrites with low lexical overlap.

Conversely, unrelated texts discussing the same topic may receive relatively high similarity scores because they share vocabulary.

Therefore, the output should be interpreted as a **quantitative similarity indicator**, not as definitive evidence of plagiarism.

## Usage

The main analysis is implemented in the Jupyter Notebook.

Open the notebook in **Google Colab** or a local Jupyter environment and run the cells sequentially.

When prompted, upload the experimental dataset in either:

* `.xlsx`
* `.csv`

format.

The notebook then:

1. loads the dataset;
2. preprocesses the texts;
3. calculates Jaccard Similarity;
4. calculates Cosine Similarity with TF-IDF;
5. computes Overall Similarity;
6. identifies shared words;
7. generates a qualitative interpretation;
8. compares results across experimental conditions;
9. performs error analysis.
